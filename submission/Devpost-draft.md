## Inspiration

A future smart bandage might observe a wound between appointments. But a changing reading is not the same thing as an infection, and a failing sensor can make an apparently confident system dangerous. I wanted to explore the harder part of autonomy: **can the control loop show its evidence and stop when that evidence is weak?**

Patchline turns that question into an interactive software prototype. The long-term idea includes a wearable patch and possible ultrasound and violet-light modules. This submission tests the *decision architecture* in a simulation; it does not test a device or a therapy.

## What it does

Patchline generates reproducible, 24-sample traces for four proposed wound-environment channels: local patch temperature, dressing wetness, fluid pH, and experimental wound-fluid glucose. Visitors can switch among a stable trace, a changing pattern, and a sensor-fault case, then adjust the final synthetic reading themselves.

A quality gate checks for missing, implausible, or isolated noisy values. If the trace passes, a small classifier trained on **separate synthetic examples** scores whether it resembles the generated stable or changing pattern. A bounded orchestrator then records the trace or prepares a human-review handoff. On the fault case, it withholds the score, requests a sensor recheck, and sets both hypothetical output indicators to zero.

The interface also depicts ultrasound and violet light as disconnected **0–3 abstract simulation tokens** so the control path is visible. They are not doses, exposure times, device settings, or treatment recommendations. No physical output exists. The handoff links its numeric claims to the samples behind them and can be printed as a report.

## How I built it

I built a dependency-free browser app with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. A seeded generator produces the virtual four-channel traces. The app trains an inspectable logistic classifier on 320 generated examples and checks it against 128 separately generated examples. These figures describe the software experiment only; the labels were generated by the simulator, not assigned by clinicians.

The processing path is deliberately explicit: **synthetic samples → sensor-quality gate → trained pattern classifier → bounded policy → evidence-linked handoff**. The interface keeps the traces, quality state, model provenance, policy decision, and source samples together. There is no backend, patient account, connected sensor, actuator interface, or runtime AI API key.

AI-assisted development helped implement the prototype. The running app's classifier is trained locally on generated data; it does not call a generative model to interpret a patient's wound.

## Challenges I ran into

The central challenge was preventing a polished simulation from sounding like clinical validation. A synthetic classifier can learn the assumptions of its generator extremely well while teaching us nothing about performance on real wounds. I separated *pattern score* from infection risk, made bad data block the score, and kept the hypothetical therapy display in arbitrary tokens with no path to hardware.

The sensor labels needed the same care. Dressing wetness is not an infection measurement, and wound-fluid glucose would not be blood glucose. Patchline names those quantities precisely and marks every trace as synthetic.

## Accomplishments that I'm proud of

- A complete, reproducible input-to-decision demo across stable, changing, and faulty traces.
- A trained model whose synthetic training and held-out evaluation are visible and whose score is withheld when the quality gate fails.
- A fault scene where an out-of-range pH sample and missing glucose reading stop the virtual control proposal.
- A printable handoff whose claims link back to exact synthetic samples.
- A candid boundary: no board, physical sensors, human data, infection diagnosis, or delivered treatment.

## What I learned

The most useful output of an autonomous health system may be a clear account of **why it did not act**. A reviewable data-quality halt is more informative than a confident number built on a broken reading. I also learned that wound-environment signals, synthetic labels, clinical infection signs, and treatment evidence must be kept distinct in the product language as well as in the code.

## What's next

The next research step is an inert benchtop sensing study: select real parts, document calibration and contact conditions, and compare traces with independent references. Any study involving people would need clinician-defined questions, human data, external validation, false-negative analysis, and appropriate oversight. Therapeutic hardware would need separate evidence of benefit and safety before any treatment policy could be proposed for human use.

## Clear use boundary

Patchline is a technical demonstration, not a medical device. It cannot diagnose infection, choose care, or treat a wound. Current [IWGDF/IDSA guidance](https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/diabetic-foot-infections/) bases diabetes-related foot-infection diagnosis on clinical assessment; temperature alone is not a diagnostic test. The proposed ultrasound and violet-light modules are research concepts, not validated treatments in this prototype. Full boundaries are in `LIMITATIONS.md` and `MODEL_CARD.md`.
